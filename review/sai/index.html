Our Verdict

Sai, from Simular, is a computer-use agent: instead of connecting to apps through APIs, it works the way a person does, clicking and typing in browsers, desktop apps, file systems and terminals. You describe a task in plain language, and it runs either on its own cloud computer (Windows or Linux, so your laptop can stay closed) or on your own Mac or Windows machine. The features built for real repeat work are recurring tasks you teach once and schedule, persistent memory of your logins and preferences, live viewing with approvals for important actions, and notifications by iMessage, SMS or Telegram. Simular also says its research agent, Agent S, was the first to beat human performance on the OSWorld benchmark — a claim about the underlying research, not a guarantee for your tasks. The trade-offs: the free plan's daily credit allowance isn't stated, Pay as you go is $50/mo for $50 of usage with cloud computer time counted against it, and the next step up is a big jump to $500/mo. Like any agent that acts on your accounts, it needs careful setup and approvals before you trust it with anything important. This verdict is built from Sai's own published pages — RankerToolAI has not run a hands-on lab test of Sai.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Sai free?

Yes, partly. The Explore plan is free with a daily credit allowance and limited access to a Windows cloud computer. Sai doesn't publish how many daily credits you get.

How much does Sai cost?

Pay as you go is $50/mo for 50,000 credits ($50 of usage), Sai Unlimited is $500/mo, and Enterprise is custom.

Does Sai need my computer to stay on?

No. Sai can run on its own cloud computer, so tasks continue after you close your laptop. You can also run it on your own Mac or Windows device.

What can Sai do?

Sai says it handles research, lead sourcing, follow-up drafting, scheduling, file organisation, spreadsheet updates, document handling and recurring reports.

Who makes Sai?

Sai is made by Simular Inc., whose research agent Agent S is described as the first to outperform human performance on the OSWorld benchmark.
