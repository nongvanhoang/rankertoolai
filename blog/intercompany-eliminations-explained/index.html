Quick Answer

When companies in the same group trade with each other, the group hasn't earned anything from outsiders, so those amounts must be removed before you add the companies together. Otherwise group revenue, costs, receivables and payables are overstated. In the example below, $6,000 of sales between two sister companies is eliminated: group sales fall from $70,500 to $64,500, and group profit doesn't change. You can do this in a spreadsheet, or set it once in a consolidation app such as Joiin, where eliminations are included on every plan.

What an Intercompany Elimination Is

Each company in a group keeps its own books. When one company sells to, lends to or charges another, both sets of books record it: a sale and a receivable on one side, a cost and a payable on the other. That's correct for each company on its own.

Consolidated reports treat the group as one business. Money moving from one pocket to another isn't income, so when you add the companies together you remove both sides of every intercompany transaction. That removal is the elimination. It only happens in the group reports; each company's own books stay as they are.

Sample figures made up for illustration.

Without the elimination, the group would report $70,500 of sales and $25,000 of cost of sales. After it, sales are $64,500 and cost of sales is $19,000. Profit is $15,950 either way, because the bakery's $6,000 of income and the coffee shop's $6,000 of cost cancel out. What changes is the size of the business you report, and every ratio built on it, such as gross margin.

If Harbor Coffee hadn't paid the bakery by month end, the bakery would show a $6,000 receivable and the coffee shop a $6,000 payable. Those are eliminated from the group balance sheet too.

What Usually Needs Eliminating

  • Sales and purchases between group companies, as in the example.
  • Management fees and recharges, such as a parent charging subsidiaries for shared staff, rent or software.
  • Intercompany balances: receivables and payables between group companies.
  • Loans and interest: the loan asset and liability, and the interest income and expense.
  • Dividends paid by a subsidiary to its parent.
  • Profit still sitting in stock: if one company sells goods to another at a markup and they haven't been sold to outside customers by period end, that unrealised profit is removed from group inventory and profit. This is where small groups most often need an accountant's help.
  • The parent's investment in a subsidiary against the subsidiary's share capital, on the balance sheet. Usually set up once, with your accountant.

How to Do It in a Spreadsheet

  1. Give intercompany transactions their own accounts in every company's ledger, such as "Sales to group companies" and "Due from Harbor Coffee". This is the step that makes everything else possible.
  2. Export each company's trial balance for the same period end and put each in its own column.
  3. Map every account to one group chart of accounts.
  4. Add an eliminations column. For each intercompany pair, enter equal and opposite amounts so both sides go to zero.
  5. Check that the eliminations column sums to zero, the intercompany accounts net to zero, and the consolidated balance sheet still balances.

Our free consolidation Excel template has the columns and checks built in. If the two sides don't match (the bakery says $6,000, the coffee shop recorded $5,800), find and fix the difference in the companies' books before you consolidate; don't force it in the eliminations column.

Common Mistakes

  • Mixing intercompany items into normal accounts. If the bakery's sale to the coffee shop sits in ordinary "Sales", nobody can find it later.
  • Eliminating only one side. Removing the sale but not the cost changes profit, which an elimination should never do on its own.
  • Different period ends or methods. One company on accrual and another on cash, or different month-ends, means the two sides won't match.
  • Currency differences. A loan in euros to a U.S. subsidiary can leave a balance that doesn't cancel after translation. Multi-currency groups need a tool or an accountant that handles exchange differences.
  • Forgetting the balance sheet. Receivables, payables and loans between companies also need eliminating, not only sales.

FAQ

Do intercompany eliminations change group profit?

A simple sale between group companies doesn't: the seller's income and the buyer's cost cancel out, so revenue and costs both fall by the same amount. Profit changes only in cases such as unrealised profit on goods still held in stock at period end.

Are eliminations recorded in each company's books?

No. Eliminations are made only in the consolidated reports. Each company's own accounts keep the intercompany sale, cost, receivable or payable as recorded.

Can I do intercompany eliminations in Excel?

Yes, for a small group with clearly labelled intercompany accounts: put each company's trial balance in its own column, add an eliminations column with equal and opposite amounts, and check it sums to zero. It becomes hard to maintain with three or more companies, foreign currencies or monthly reporting.
