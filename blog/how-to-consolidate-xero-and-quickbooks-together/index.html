How to Consolidate Xero and QuickBooks Companies Together

Guide by Hoang Nong, RankerToolAI · November 28, 2026 · Worked example with sample figures, based on Xero, QuickBooks Online, Joiin and Fathom documentation, not a client engagement. See our methodology.

Quick Answer

You don't need to move either company onto the other system. Neither Xero nor QuickBooks Online can report across companies, so you consolidate outside both: export each trial balance into one spreadsheet, map both charts of accounts to one shared list of group lines, then remove intercompany sales and balances. If you do this every month, a consolidation app that connects to both systems (such as Joiin or Fathom) does the mapping once and refreshes the numbers for you.

The example: one company on Xero, one on QuickBooks

We use the same sample group as our intercompany eliminations guide, with one change: the two companies run different ledgers. This is a common setup. A group buys a company that already has its own books, or a founder set up the second company years after the first and chose a different system.

  • Harbor Coffee Ltd keeps its books in Xero. October sales $42,000; cost of sales from outside suppliers $9,800; it also bought $6,000 of bread from Harbor Bakery.
  • Harbor Bakery Ltd keeps its books in QuickBooks Online. October sales $28,500, of which $6,000 went to Harbor Coffee; cost of sales $9,200.
  • Both report in US dollars and both are 100% owned by Harbor Group. The figures are sample numbers, not a real company.

The goal is one group profit and loss that shows what the group earned from outside customers: sales of $64,500, not the $70,500 you get by adding the two companies together.

Why the two charts of accounts don't line up

Adding the two trial balances line by line doesn't work, because Xero and QuickBooks Online name and group accounts differently. Each account belongs to a built-in account type, and the types don't match one to one:

In our example, the same economic line appears under different names. Harbor Coffee books its sales to 200 Sales (type Revenue) and its purchases to 310 Cost of Goods Sold (type Direct Costs). Harbor Bakery books its sales to Sales of Product Income (type Income) and its purchases to Cost of Goods Sold. Whichever route you take, you build one mapping that sends each account in each company to one group line. You build it once and only touch it again when someone adds an account.

Before you start: 4 fixes in each ledger

  1. Give intercompany activity its own accounts on both sides. In Xero, add an account such as 320 Purchases from Harbor Bakery. In QuickBooks, add Sales to Harbor Coffee and an Intercompany receivable account. Then the eliminations are easy to find every month.
  2. Agree the intercompany figures before you close. If the bakery invoiced $6,000 but the coffee shop recorded $5,800, fix it in the books. Don't plug the difference in the consolidation.
  3. Close the same period in both systems. Set a lock date in Xero and close the books in QuickBooks Online for the month, so the numbers you consolidate stop changing.
  4. Match the year-end and the reporting basis. Both companies should report on the same financial year and the same basis (accrual or cash), or the totals won't mean anything.

Option A: consolidate in a spreadsheet

For two companies and one report a quarter, a spreadsheet is enough. Our free consolidation Excel template already has the columns below.

  1. Export a trial balance from each ledger for the same date. In Xero: Accounting › Reports › Trial Balance, then export to Excel. In QuickBooks Online: Reports › Trial Balance, then export to Excel.
  2. Paste each export into its own tab and add a "Group line" column next to every account, using the table above.
  3. Sum by group line with SUMIF, one column per company.
  4. Add an eliminations column and remove the intercompany sale from both sides, plus any intercompany receivable and payable still open at month end.
  5. Check that the result still balances, and that group profit equals the two companies' profits added together (a simple intercompany sale doesn't change profit).

Option B: connect both to a consolidation app

A consolidation app sits on top of both ledgers. Each company keeps its own system, and the app reads the data through each system's connection. In Joiin, the setup for our example looks like this:

  1. Connect Harbor Coffee from Xero and Harbor Bakery from QuickBooks Online. Joiin imports both charts of accounts and maps each account to its own account types (Revenue, Variable COS, Accounts Receivable and so on).
  2. Review the mapping once. Check that Sales to Harbor Coffee and 200 Sales both land on revenue, and fix any account that landed on the wrong type. Joiin's AI chat can also scan the chart for misclassified accounts.
  3. Set up the elimination once, by account, by contact or both. Here, that means Bakery's Sales to Harbor Coffee against Coffee's 320 Purchases from Harbor Bakery. Joiin then applies it to every report.
  4. Run the group P&L and compare it by company, with eliminations shown in their own column. Joiin refreshes both companies' data daily, so next month you open the report instead of rebuilding it.

If the two companies use different currencies

Mixed ledgers often come with mixed currencies, for example a UK parent on Xero in pounds and a US subsidiary on QuickBooks in dollars. Before you add anything up, convert the profit and loss at the average rate for the period and the balance sheet at the month-end rate. The difference goes to a translation reserve in equity.

In a spreadsheet, that means a rates tab and two extra columns per company. In Joiin, multi-currency is on the Pro and Max plans: it uses month-end and monthly average rates by default, and you can enter your own rates. Fathom Pro includes multi-currency consolidation.

FAQ

Can you consolidate a Xero company with a QuickBooks Online company?

Yes, but not inside either ledger. Export both trial balances to a spreadsheet and map them to shared group lines, or connect both companies to a consolidation app such as Joiin or Fathom, which map the accounts and apply eliminations for you.

Do I have to use the same chart of accounts in both systems?

No. You map each company's accounts to one list of group lines. Matching names and codes where you can makes the mapping faster, and separate intercompany accounts on both sides make eliminations easy to find.

How do I remove sales between the two companies?

Record them in dedicated intercompany accounts in each ledger, agree the amounts before close, then reverse both sides in the consolidation: the seller's intercompany sales and the buyer's intercompany purchases, plus any open receivable and payable.

What does it cost to consolidate Xero and QuickBooks with an app?

On November 28, 2026, Joiin's price calculator showed $41 a month, or $32 a month billed annually, for two companies on the Core plan, and $45 or $36 on Pro, which adds multi-currency. Fathom Pro starts at $59 a month for one company.

Can QuickBooks Desktop be part of the group?

Joiin's list of connections names QuickBooks Online. Fathom says it connects to QuickBooks Desktop as well as Online. With Joiin, a company on a system it doesn't connect to can still be added by uploading its data.
