When Excel Is Enough

A spreadsheet is a sensible starting point if all of these are true:

  • Two companies, both on the same accounting system and the same year end.
  • One reporting currency.
  • Few or no transactions between the companies.
  • You consolidate quarterly or yearly, not every month.
  • One person maintains the file and knows how it works.

Five Signs You've Outgrown the Spreadsheet

  1. A third company. Every new entity adds another export, another set of mappings and more intercompany pairs to reconcile.
  2. Regular intercompany trading. Sales, recharges or loans between companies every month mean eliminations every month. See intercompany eliminations explained.
  3. A foreign subsidiary. Translating at average and closing rates by hand is where workbooks break.
  4. A monthly board pack. If owners or lenders expect group numbers within days of month end, copy-paste becomes the bottleneck.
  5. Key-person risk. If only one person can update the file, a holiday or resignation stops group reporting.

What Software Does That Excel Doesn't

In our walkthrough of Joiin's free trial with two sample companies, the steps that replace the spreadsheet were:

  • Add each company once, by connecting its ledger or uploading a trial balance.
  • Run the P&L compared by company, with one column per company and a group total.
  • Create an elimination group (Settings → Eliminations) pairing one company's intercompany sales with the other's purchases. Joiin showed whether the pair balanced, and the report could show eliminations in their own columns.
  • Save the report into a report pack to present, share, send or export to Excel or PDF.

How to Switch Without Losing Your Workbook

  1. Keep your last closed month in Excel as the reference.
  2. Start the trial and add the same companies for the same month.
  3. Recreate your group chart of accounts mapping and your intercompany pairs.
  4. Compare revenue, gross profit, net profit and the balance sheet totals with the workbook. Investigate any difference before trusting either.
  5. Run one more month in parallel, then retire the spreadsheet or keep it only for one-off analysis.

FAQ

Can Excel handle multi-entity consolidation?

Yes, for a small group: two companies on similar charts of accounts, one currency and few intercompany transactions. You export each trial balance, map accounts to a group chart, add an eliminations column and check that the balance sheet balances. It gets fragile with more companies, foreign currencies or a monthly close.

How many companies before consolidation software is worth it?

Usually three or more, or two if you trade between them every month, have a foreign subsidiary or need a monthly board pack. The deciding factor is the time spent rebuilding the spreadsheet each period.

How much does consolidation software cost for two companies?

On November 28, 2026, Joiin's price calculator showed $41 a month for two companies on its Core plan with monthly billing, or $32 a month billed annually, with unlimited users and a 14-day free trial. Other tools price by organisation or tier, so compare at your own company count.

Can I keep using my Excel template after switching?

Yes. Many teams keep the workbook as a reference for one or two months while they check the software's totals, and some keep Excel for one-off analysis using exported reports.
