How to Translate Training & Marketing Videos into Other Languages with AI

Localize one video into many languages without hiring voice actors for each market — and know when a native speaker still needs to check it.

TL;DR: For new videos, build once in Synthesia and switch the script language (140+ languages). For existing footage, use AI dubbing (Synthesia offers dubbing into 70+ languages on Basic, Starter and Pro). Always have a native speaker review the final script.

Recording the same video for every market is expensive: voice talent, studio time and editing for each language. AI changes the cost structure — the expensive part becomes translation review, not production.

Two Ways to Localize a Video

Option A: Rebuild with a multilingual AI presenter

Best for training, onboarding and explainers you control end to end. Build the video once in Synthesia, then duplicate it and replace the script with a translated version. The avatar presents it in the new language with matching lip movement. Synthesia supports 140+ languages and voices. On Enterprise, 1-click translation automates the duplicate-and-translate step; on other plans you paste translated scripts yourself.

Option B: Dub existing footage

Best when you already have filmed video, such as webinars, founder videos or YouTube content. AI dubbing translates the speech and generates a voice in the target language. Synthesia lists dubbing into 70+ languages on its Basic, Starter and Pro plans; ElevenLabs is the common alternative for voice-focused dubbing.

A Localization Workflow That Holds Up

  1. Finalize the source script first. Every change after translation multiplies across languages.
  2. Translate with AI, review with a human. Machine translation gets you 90% there; a native speaker should check product terms, tone and anything legal.
  3. Check on-screen text. Titles, captions and screenshots need translating too, not only the voice.
  4. Render one language, review, then batch the rest. Catch layout issues (longer German text, right-to-left Arabic) before producing ten versions.
  5. Name files clearly (e.g. onboarding-01_es.mp4) so the right version reaches the right team.

Budget tip: every language version uses video minutes. A 5-minute module in 6 languages is about 30 minutes of generation, which is why multilingual teams usually outgrow Starter (~12 min/mo) and land on Pro (~60 min/mo).

Where AI Translation Still Falls Short

  • Humor, idioms and wordplay rarely survive translation; rewrite them per market.
  • Less common languages can have less natural voices than English, Spanish or French. Preview before committing.
  • Regulated content (medical, financial, safety) needs a qualified human reviewer in each language.
