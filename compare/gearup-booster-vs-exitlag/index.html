GearUP Booster vs ExitLag (2026)

Short Answer

Choose ExitLag if you play on Windows or mobile and want a published price list ($6.25 a month on the annual plan) and a 7-day money-back guarantee. Choose GearUP Booster if you play on console or want the boost at router level for the whole home: it covers PlayStation, Xbox and Switch and sells a dedicated router plan. Neither will fix lag caused by weak Wi-Fi or an overloaded game server.

Pricing

ExitLag publishes its prices: $9.99 a month, $8.33 a month on the quarterly plan and $6.25 a month on the annual plan, which its checkout totals at $75 for the year (against $119.88 at the monthly rate).

GearUP does not publish a standard price list, and the price you see can depend on your region and platform. The rates reported for its PC membership run from about $4.49 to $9.99 a month, about $29.99 for three months and about $79.99 to $99 a year. GearUP's shop lists Mobile Membership at $6.99 a month and Router Membership at $9.99 a month.

On paper the two are close on an annual plan. The real difference is certainty: with ExitLag you know the price before you sign up, while with GearUP you see it during the trial checkout. See our GearUP pricing breakdown for the full range.

Trial and Refunds

This is where ExitLag is easier on the buyer. Its pricing page shows a 7-day money-back guarantee on its plans.

GearUP offers a 3-day free trial, but it requires a payment method and renews into a paid plan unless you cancel. Its user agreement makes purchases final and non-refundable by default. Residents of the EEA, Switzerland and the UK can ask for a refund within 14 days of the first transaction, possibly reduced for use, and residents of South Korea can ask at any time with a 10% fee after 7 days. If you buy through an app store, that store's rules also apply.

Put simply: with GearUP, test inside the 3-day trial and cancel if it doesn't help. With ExitLag, you can pay and still ask for your money back within 7 days.

Platforms and Consoles

GearUP covers more devices. It lists support for over 3,000 games across PC, iOS, Android and consoles including PlayStation, Xbox and Nintendo Switch. For consoles and whole-home use it sells a Router Membership and HYPEREV, a dedicated gaming router sold as a one-time hardware purchase.

ExitLag's pricing page lists Windows, Android and iOS. If you play on a console, GearUP is the one of the two built for it.

Who Should Choose Which

  • PC player who wants to know the price upfront: ExitLag, for its published plans and 7-day money-back guarantee.
  • Console player (PlayStation, Xbox, Switch): GearUP Booster, for console support and the router option.
  • Household with several devices: GearUP, whose Router Membership and HYPEREV router apply the routing to the whole network.
  • Mobile player: both cover iOS and Android; compare GearUP's $6.99 Mobile Membership with ExitLag's plans.
  • Lag from Wi-Fi or a crowded home network: neither. Try a wired connection first.

How to Test Before You Commit

  • Pick one game and one server region, and note your ping and packet loss without any booster for a few sessions at the hours you usually play.
  • Run the same sessions with the booster on. A booster helps only if the route between you and the server is the problem.
  • With GearUP, set a reminder to cancel before the 3-day trial ends. With ExitLag, keep your test inside the 7-day money-back window.
  • Check the game's terms. Both tools reroute network traffic rather than edit game files, but neither publishes an anti-cheat guarantee for every title.

FAQ

Is ExitLag better than GearUP Booster?

Neither is better for everyone. ExitLag publishes its prices and offers a 7-day money-back guarantee, which suits PC and mobile players. GearUP covers consoles and sells a router-level plan, which suits console players and households.

Which is cheaper, GearUP Booster or ExitLag?

They are close on an annual plan: ExitLag is $75 a year ($6.25 a month) and GearUP's reported annual rate is about $79.99 to $99. GearUP doesn't publish a standard price list, so check the price at checkout for your region.

Does ExitLag work on PS5 or Xbox?

ExitLag's pricing page lists Windows, Android and iOS. GearUP lists PlayStation, Xbox and Nintendo Switch support, plus a Router Membership and the HYPEREV router for console setups.

Does GearUP Booster have a refund policy?

Purchases are non-refundable by default under GearUP's user agreement. The EEA, Switzerland and the UK get a 14-day refund window from the first transaction, and South Korea can ask at any time with a 10% fee after 7 days.

Do I need a card for the GearUP free trial?

Yes. GearUP's 3-day trial requires a payment method and renews into a paid plan unless you cancel before it ends.

Will a game booster fix high ping?

Only when the problem is the network route between you and the game server. A booster won't fix weak Wi-Fi, a busy home connection or an overloaded server, so test with and without it on the same server.
